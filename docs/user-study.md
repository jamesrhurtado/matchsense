## Preparation and consent

Assign anonymous IDs P01/V01. Obtain consent separately for participation, recording, anonymized quotation and public screenshots. Store consent, names/contact details and raw recordings outside the public repository. Participants may stop without explanation. Public reporting uses anonymous observations only.

Pilot once, revise confusing tasks, then record an instrument version and freeze it before timed sessions. Have an independent football reviewer prepare the answer key from observations; use a competent Spanish reviewer for Spanish meaning. Select distinct matched scenes for baseline and candidate. Counterbalance order AB/BA and scene assignment; record assignment before the session. Do not reuse a scene to make the second interface easier.

Use silent timed tasks followed by retrospective discussion. Start each timer when the participant can see the scene and instructions; stop at their completed decision. Record requests for help, all failed attempts and timeouts. Explain navigation before testing, but do not explain the intended football insight. Discovery think-aloud sessions are separately labeled and excluded from clean timing comparisons.

## Opening script

“We are testing the interface and explanations. You are not being tested. Some passages may not support a useful explanation. Use the information available, and tell us when you cannot conclude something. You can stop at any time. Please work through each task without talking through your reasoning; we will discuss it afterward.”

## Producer tasks

1. Watch the continuous replay and decide whether any passage is worth showing to a viewer. Review or decline the proposed explanation.
2. Find the supporting evidence. State what you can establish and one thing it does not establish.
3. Review a proposal explicitly labeled **research challenge: intentionally unsupported statement**. Correct or reject it. Never describe this staged challenge as a spontaneous AI failure.
4. Approve a supported revision while replay continues. Record evidence readiness, proposal readiness, task start, approval and browser appearance as separate times; count expiry as a failure.
5. Find an earlier approved moment and retract it while preserving the other approval.

Observer captures: completion without help; wrong or accidental publication; requested help; cosmetic/substantive edit; time; missing/late proposal; whether participant would show it, and why. Define substantive edits before rating: changing actor, polarity, measurement, qualification, claim, or audience meaning; spelling/punctuation alone is cosmetic.

## Viewer tasks

1. Explain in your own words what happened and what changed.
2. Identify the passer and actual receiver. Describe who kept possession afterward.
3. State one conclusion the displayed evidence does not establish.
4. Switch between simple and detailed views and locate a measurement and its observation window.
5. Focus on a player, find a relevant approved moment, then interpret an empty involvement state.
6. Open the recap after a moment is retracted and describe what changed.

Freeze each scene's answer key before the session: expected entities/team identities, supported predicate and time window, team versus individual retention, numerical tolerance and required qualifications. Accept equivalent plain language. Record incorrect beliefs verbatim with consent. A polished paraphrase cannot repair a participant's wrong understanding in the results.

## Retrospective prompts and rubric

Ask: “What did you use to make that decision?”, “What was unclear?”, “What would you change before using this?”, and “What did the evidence control change about your confidence?” Avoid asking whether the AI was impressive.

Rate factual understanding, specificity, audience relevance and editorial usefulness separately from 1 to 5: 1 = wrong/unusable or empty generic content; 3 = accurate and understandable but routine; 5 = concise, specific, supported and useful. Severe false belief or accidental publication is a separate critical flag and overrides average scores. A second reviewer grades all critical/suspicious cases plus at least 25% of the rest; record disagreements and adjudication. Arm labels stay hidden during content grading.
