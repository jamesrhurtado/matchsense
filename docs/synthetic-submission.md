# Synthetic data for the hackathon submission

## What we made

MatchSense uses original, fictional football scenes to show how the product
turns a stream of match observations into evidence-backed stories. The contest
asks for synthetic, football-realistic events and explainable match intelligence;
it does not ask us to simulate a complete league or claim the scenes represent a
real-match population.
Our goal is a small set of understandable examples that make evidence, uncertainty
and corrections visible.

The **judge-facing replay** is the separately versioned, one-minute fixture
`match-0c0a000000000009`, generated by `simulator/short_demo.py`. The larger
constrained-development slice in `docs/synthetic-dataset-v1.md` is opt-in
development material. Its cases and expected answers are not the default demo
catalogue. This keeps presentation data distinct in purpose from development
and regression data.

## How the scenes are generated

- Each scene is authored as fictional player path knots, ball/control intervals,
  and source events. One shared episode state drives the ball, controller,
  event timing and event coordinates.
- The constrained development scenes use a 105 × 68 m pitch at 10 Hz. Coordinates,
  axis direction, period attack direction and player roles are explicit.
- Player paths use quintic interpolation with continuous position, velocity and
  acceleration at authored knots. A versioned profile applies movement bounds.
  These are generator design choices, not physiological limits or FIFA certification.
- The generator is deterministic and does not use real tracking, footage, model
  calls or random seeds. Authored paths are scripts, not learned tactics or a full
  match simulator.
- The runtime stream contains observations and source events. Expected answers,
  authoring paths and scenario ancestry stay in development files. A manifest records
  the generator/profile versions and content hashes.

## Two controlled examples

These use existing authored scenes; they do not add another data suite.

| Example | What is held constant | What changes | What the demo illustrates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Support pass, `d01-a` / `d01-b` | Same authored passage and player paths | In `d01-b`, one nearby opponent is missing from the 18–20 s observation window | Story eligibility should respond to available evidence, rather than treating a missing opponent as proof of pressure or no pressure. |
| Goal correction, `d04-a` / `d04-b` | Same authored shot, initial score and goal confirmation | `d04-b` adds a later source cancellation | A confirmed story can be withdrawn when its source is corrected; the displayed score and recap should follow the latest source state. |

The opt-in development catalogue remains separate from the judge-facing one-minute
replay. These examples are available for inspection in the development workflow,
not positioned as additional submission demos.

## Compact quality snapshot

| Layer | Current evidence | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Traceability | Six authored scene parents; 12 specifications; 24 source views; deterministic generation and hashed provenance | Independent or real-match ancestry |
| Movement and geometry | Metric pitch and 10 Hz sampling; shared ball/control state; C2 paths; sampled acceleration maximum about 5.68 m/s² against the declared 6 m/s² design bound | Human movement distributions, tactical realism or universal limits |
| Event and source coherence | Source-only validation passes 24/24 views; alignment checks cover contacts, control, shots, goals, corrections and timing | That all football events or laws are modeled |
| Demonstrated behaviors | 32/32 authored checkpoints pass across the six scene families, including quiet play, incomplete observations and corrections | Real-world detector accuracy, audience benefit or production readiness |

These are checks against declared construction choices and expected behaviors. They
are evidence that the fixture is reproducible and behaves as designed, not a single
“realism score.”

For the separate one-minute presentation replay, both keepers are in their own
penalty areas at the inspected 00:00, 00:20, 00:40 and 00:59.9 samples. At 00:20
their x coordinates are about 6.45 m and 101.49 m on the 105 m pitch. This is a
spot check of that fixture only; the paths are scripted near-goal lanes, not learned
or validated keeper tactics.

## Submission-ready description

> We created a small, deterministic synthetic football dataset from original
> fictional scenes. A shared episode state keeps player paths, ball control and
> event coordinates synchronized, while metric coordinates, sampling rate,
> interpolation and movement bounds are documented. Controlled pairs show how
> missing observations affect a pressure-related story and how a later source
> correction withdraws a confirmed goal story. We keep the one-minute presentation
> replay separate from the larger development/regression slice, store expected
> answers outside runtime observations, and record versioned provenance. Our checks
> establish consistency with these authored scenarios; they do not claim a complete
> or statistically representative football simulator.

## Research basis and boundaries

This approach follows the hackathon's request for synthetic football-realistic
events and explainable match intelligence ([official rules](https://github.com/microsoft/insidethegamehackathon/blob/main/OFFICIAL%20RULES.md)).
It borrows practical data-quality habits from [ISO/IEC 5259-2](https://www.iso.org/standard/81860.html?browse=tc)
(define measurable, purpose-specific quality characteristics), the [NIST AI RMF](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/)
(document suitability and representativeness limits), [Datasheets for Datasets](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/datasheets-for-datasets/)
(state composition, creation and intended use), and [W3C PROV](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-overview/)
(trace entities and derivations). These are design references, not certification
claims.

Football-specific decisions are informed by the [IFAB Laws](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/),
[FIFA tracking quality guidance](https://inside.fifa.com/en/innovation/news/new-global-standard-for-epts),
and [FIFA goalkeeper positioning guidance](https://www.fifatrainingcentre.com/en/practice/elite-sessions/goalkeeper/in_and_out_of_possession.php).
They inform event distinctions, explicit units, observation limits and contextual
keeper movement; they do not turn scripted paths into validated tactical behavior.
No external trajectories, footage, player identities or club marks were imported.

For the repo-level source audit and fuller limitations, see
[`synthetic-data-research.md`](synthetic-data-research.md) and the
[`synthetic-dataset-v1.md`](synthetic-dataset-v1.md) dataset card.
