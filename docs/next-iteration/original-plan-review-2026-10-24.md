## Main assessment

The implementation has delivered much of the **trustworthy production workflow**:
observations, deterministic facts, evidence-bound explanations, producer approval,
viewer delivery, personalization and recap. The strongest remaining gap is the
**editorial promise**: making a viewer immediately understand what changed and why
this particular moment deserves attention. Event coverage and factual support are
more mature than match-level importance, tactical interpretation or user validation.

The original idea was studio-first: one important observed sequence becomes a
studio cue, an overlay, a simple fan explanation, a deeper analyst explanation,
a player view and a multilingual recap. Its distinctive feature was the evidence
chain tying those outputs to the same facts. The larger infrastructure and agent
roster were means to that outcome, not the outcome itself.

## A concrete gap in the main demo

A read-only check of the new moving discovery fixture (`...0003`) through
`pass_metrics` and `contextual_metrics` produced:

| Pass | History status | Prior comparators |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | insufficient_history | 0 |
| 2 | insufficient_history | 1 |
| 3 | insufficient_history | 1 |
| 4 | insufficient_history | 1 |

Its pass times are too far apart to supply three preceding completed passes
inside the defined two-minute population. Therefore this flagship cannot show
our existing historical-comparison feature, even though that feature works.
The separate **Previous-pass history A** fixture (`match-57c134ad6e6457a4`)
produces `greater_than_each` for pass 4 with three comparators. History B supplies
the contrasting case where the same current pass should not receive that claim.

This is a scenario-selection/integration gap, not a reason to loosen thresholds
or manufacture comparison text. The new moving fixture repairs motion and event
breadth, but does not yet deliver the original single coherent turning-point demo.

## What is worth including next

1. **One integrated flagship sequence.** Bring coherent motion, enough authentic
   recent-pass history, mixed event coverage, one source-backed before/after
   change, approval and audience adaptation into one versioned segment. Keep the
   independently authored contrast/negative cases. Explain observed progression,
   changed proximity or a supported comparison; do not call it an intentional
   press trap or claim a pass caused a goal without the necessary observations.
2. **Human-readable evidence before audit codes.** Present who/action/time,
   before → event → observed result, actual interval and coverage. Keep IDs,
   revisions and hashes in a labeled technical audit disclosure. The audit data
   already exists; the current plain event-evidence panel makes users decode it.
3. **Clearly different audience outputs.** Demonstrate the same approved moment
   side by side: a short casual sentence, an analyst view with measurements,
   baselines and qualifications, and a role-focused player explanation. Keep
   Spanish meaning/role equivalence. Do not manufacture an off-ball contribution.
4. **Visible, measured AI execution for the release being shown.** Existing pass
   model integration and discovery results are real recorded work. Today's local
   demo is deterministic. The broad-family v5 adapter has fake-provider/offline
   checks and remains experimental against an actual deployment. Verify the
   target model and preserve usage/failure/fallback records before presenting
   that path as model-backed. A repairable rejection/fallback is useful evidence,
   not something to hide from the demo.
5. **Real first-time tasks and football review.** Use the prepared pilot with
   matched/reviewer-frozen keys. Can a participant distinguish the score from
   editorial approval, understand why a story appeared, inspect its support and
   interpret the possession interval? Record failures as well as success. Owner
   feedback is valuable, but is not the planned recruited study or ROI evidence.
6. **A version-consistent final delivery.** Freeze the demonstrated source/fixtures,
   verify the same features on the judging endpoint, and prepare a video under
   two minutes as the original plan specifies. The previous three-minute guide
   is a rehearsal walkthrough, not the final submission-video cut. Repository
   publication, deployment and external submission remain separate authorized
   actions; none was performed for this review.

## Useful additions not explicit enough in the initial plan

- Separate **Why this moment?** (supported editorial relevance/comparison) from
  **Check the evidence** (factual provenance). The original Why button blurred
  importance with proof; the owner's questions expose that distinction.
- Use evidence states such as complete/limited/awaiting outcome, with a declared
  interval and coverage. Avoid a decorative confidence percentage: the original
  plan proposes confidence, but there is no calibrated probability of importance.
- Add an intelligible story-eligibility/playback view: playing, waiting for an
  outcome, ready for review, approved, archived or corrected. Guided pauses are
  already a useful start; keep them clearly separate from automated publication.
- Add a small, documented editorial noise policy—freshness, duplication, bounded
  comparative relevance and suppression/cooldown—tested with reviewers. Current
  family priority is transparent but does not measure usefulness.
- Maintain a release/demo capability checklist: this exact URL, mode, model,
  feature family and fallback behavior were verified. Judges should see the
  same build being described, rather than an older hosted pass-only slice.

## Valuable work already added beyond the original proposal

Confirmed score separated from approval; cancellations and corrected recaps;
explicit shot outcomes and unknown actors/locations; possession intervals,
denominators and missing-data coverage; mixed-event approval/history; exact
package compatibility across versions; local zero-call mode; duplicate/out-of-order
and restart behavior; visible carrier/keeper identification; guided review and
navigation to evidence. These make the underlying product more defensible than a
single dramatic scripted narrative, even though they need a clearer presentation.

## What I would leave out now

Do not add Fabric, extra scouts, graph storage, a general chatbot, CV, speech,
vector memory, sponsorship or speculative counterfactuals merely to mirror the
research report. Do not rename the present geometric heuristics as Chaos Index,
pass difficulty or line breaking. The next improvement should make one useful,
well-supported story obvious to a new viewer and demonstrate it working through
the entire pipeline.
