# MatchSense football expert review manual

## Your assignment

Help us determine whether MatchSense gives a useful, supported explanation of a football moment. Review the supplied observations and anonymous explanations independently. We need your judgment, including reasons to withhold or reject a story.

Session length: 40 to 45 minutes. Instrument: R1, 22 October 2026.

## Who this review is for

You should have practical knowledge of football analysis through coaching, scouting, performance analysis, football journalism or commentary. You should be able to distinguish an observed action from an interpretation of its tactical value. A formal qualification is helpful but not required; explain your experience and limits. No AI or software expertise is needed.

## What you need to know

MatchSense presents explanations of football passages, supporting evidence and different viewing preferences. This session uses synthetic observations and fictional players. Treat the supplied evidence as the material being assessed; identify when it cannot support a conclusion. We are testing the product, not your football knowledge.

Explanations may use different generation methods. Their identities stay hidden during your review. Some outputs may look similar, contain mistakes or show no explanation. Evaluate each on its own merits before comparing them. Do not look up the project, watch its demonstration, or discuss answers with another reviewer before submitting.

## Preparation and participation

- Use a laptop or desktop with a readable screen. Bring glasses or other usual accessibility tools if needed.
- The facilitator supplies the session link, anonymous output IDs, source evidence and a response sheet for each output. Ask for missing material before starting.
- Work independently. Read normally; do not narrate during any timed task. We will discuss your reasoning afterward.
- You may ask for help, pause or stop. Help and uncertainty are useful observations.
- Participation, recording and public use of quotations are separate choices. The facilitator will ask about each. Recording is optional.

## Session tasks

Allow 5 minutes for introduction, 25 minutes for four anonymous outputs, 10 minutes for discussion and 5 minutes for closing. The facilitator may reduce the number of outputs if careful evidence review takes longer.

### F1 Understand the evidence

Inspect the supplied replay or observation summary before reading the first explanation. Describe what happened, who was involved and what you can establish. State what remains uncertain. Ask for a plain-language explanation of an unfamiliar measurement rather than assuming its meaning.

### F2 Review each explanation independently

Read one assigned output and check its statements against the supplied evidence. Complete a separate response sheet on the next page. Identify exact wording and source references where possible. Do not assume that an attractive explanation is correct or that a short explanation is incomplete.

For any comparison, explain which actions are being compared, what is measured and which time period is covered. Record whether the wording could cause a viewer to infer something the evidence does not establish.

### F3 Decide whether this deserves attention

Would you highlight this moment for a football audience? Explain why, what context would be needed and whether the explanation adds anything useful. Identify the intended audience if your judgment depends on their football knowledge.

If no explanation is provided, judge whether staying quiet is appropriate. If the reason cannot be determined from the material, say so. Do not invent missing content or treat every absence as a correct decision.

### F4 Compare only after individual ratings

Once every assigned sheet is complete, compare the outputs for the same passage. Ties and no preference are valid. Choose the most useful supported explanation, if any, and explain which specific wording or omission decided your preference.
